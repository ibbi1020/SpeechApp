This policy explains what Orator collects, what stays on your phone, and who else receives your speech. Orator is a speech-practice app. Reading and topic talks work without an account. A live conversation with the AI partner requires an account and a subscription.

We do not sell your information. We do not rent it. We do not help other companies advertise to you.

## 1. Who this covers

This policy covers the Orator app on iPhone. It does not cover Apple, Google, or xAI. Their policies cover what they do after information reaches them.

You must be 18 or older. Orator is not for children. We do not ask for your date of birth. Before a live conversation, we ask you to confirm that you are 18 or older, and we store the time of that confirmation.

## 2. Information you give us

**Sign-in.** You can sign in with Apple or with Google. We receive a stable identifier from that provider. We receive your email address only if that provider shares it. Hide My Email and a missing email are both fine. We do not store your name. We do not store a photo. Signing in with a second provider does not merge two accounts, even when the email address is the same.

**Age confirmation.** We store the time you confirmed that you are 18 or older. We do not store a birth date.

**Time zone.** We store your time zone so the monthly conversation limit follows your calendar, not a single world clock.

**Practice numbers, only if you turn sync on.** Sync is off until you opt in. After you opt in, we store counts from your practice: for example pace, pauses, filled pauses, words matched or missed, and how long you spoke. We do not store the recording. We do not store the transcript. Another phone signed into the same account can show those counts. It cannot play the audio.

If you turn sync off, we stop accepting new counts. Counts already stored stay until you delete the account. Practice you do while sync is off stays on that phone and is not uploaded later.

**Messages you send us.** If you email us, we keep that email so we can reply.

## 3. Speech

**What leaves the phone.** When you read aloud, talk on a topic, listen back to a topic talk, or speak with the AI partner, the audio is sent to xAI so the app can turn speech into words or hold the live conversation. Reading, topic talks, and listen-back use xAI speech-to-text. The live conversation uses xAI’s voice model. The partner is software. It is not a person.

Reading, topic talks, and listen-back pass through our server on the way to xAI. The server copies the audio across and does not save it. A live conversation does not take that path. The phone receives a short-lived token and sends that audio to xAI directly.

xAI’s published description of its voice API says audio is processed in real time and is not stored or used to train models. We do not control xAI’s systems. xAI’s consumer privacy policy for the Grok app does not apply to this API.

**What stays on the phone.** Topic-talk recordings and transcripts stay on the phone that made them. They are not uploaded. They are not copied to iCloud by the app. Deleting the app deletes them. We cannot restore them.

Pace, pauses, and review marks are calculated on the phone from the words xAI returns.

If xAI cannot be reached, reading and topic talks can fall back to Apple’s on-device speech recognition. That fallback does not send the audio to us. The live conversation does not have this fallback. It does not start unless our service can reach xAI.

**What we do not do with speech.** We do not keep your audio. We do not keep your transcript. We do not build a voiceprint. We do not use your voice to identify you. We do not use your speech to train our own models. We do not sell it.

**Crisis.** If a conversation ends because the app shows a crisis screen, we add one to a daily count. That count is not tied to your account. It does not include what was said. The screen tells you how to reach the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline in the United States, and to use your local emergency number if you are somewhere else.

## 4. Information we receive from others

**Apple and Google** send the sign-in identifier, and an email address only when they share one. Apple can later tell us to forget an email address, or that you revoked the app or deleted your Apple Account. We act on that notice.

**Apple** handles the subscription payment. We never see your card number. Apple tells us whether the subscription is active, when it renews or expires, and the transaction id. We store that status so the app can allow or refuse a live conversation. The phone cannot grant itself a subscription.

## 5. Information we do not collect

We do not collect your contacts, photos, precise location, advertising identifier, or date of birth. We do not use a crash reporter. If we add one, we will update this policy first. We do not use cookies. The app has no third-party advertising.

## 6. How we use information

We use account and subscription information to sign you in, to enforce the monthly conversation limit, and to keep one live conversation from running twice at the same time.

We use practice numbers, when you have opted in, to show your progress on the phones where you are signed in.

We use the age confirmation to refuse a live conversation when you have not confirmed that you are 18 or older.

We use messages you send us to answer you.

We do not use your information to train a model. We do not use it for advertising.

## 7. Who we share information with

We share information with service providers only so they can perform the function named here.

| Who | What they receive | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| xAI | The audio of a practice or a live conversation, while it is happening | Transcription and the live AI partner |
| Apple | Sign in with Apple, and the subscription payment | Account and payment. Apple already has your Apple Account |
| Google | Sign in with Google, if you choose it | Account. Google already has your Google account |
| Cloudflare | The account row, the subscription status, and practice numbers if sync is on. Also the live audio of a reading, a topic talk, or listen-back, only while it is passed to xAI | Hosts our database and our API. Does not save that audio or the transcript. Does not receive live-conversation audio |

We may also disclose information if the law requires it, or to protect someone’s safety. A sale or merger of the app could transfer the same records this policy describes. It would not start a new use of your speech.

We do not share practice numbers or account identifiers with xAI. If a later xAI setting requires a stable id, we will send a one-way hash that we do not store, and we will update this policy first.

## 8. How long we keep it

Account information stays until you delete the account.

Practice numbers stay until you delete the account. Turning sync off does not delete them.

Speech is not kept by us. Recordings on the phone stay until you delete them, delete the app, or choose to erase them when you delete the account.

After you delete the account, we may keep the App Store transaction id, the product id, and whether it was a production purchase. That record has no account id and no name. We keep it for tax, refunds, and charge disputes. [Counsel: set the purge period.]

The daily crisis count stays. It was never linked to you.

Sign-in tokens are revoked with the provider when you delete the account.

## 9. Your choices

**Sync.** You can leave practice sync off. Reading and topic talks still work.

**Recordings.** You can delete a recording on the phone. We have no copy to delete.

**Account.** You can delete the account in the app. We revoke the Apple or Google sign-in, then delete the account, the subscription mirror, and the practice numbers. A second prompt asks whether to delete recordings on that phone. Another phone does not erase its recordings in the background. The next time it opens and the account is gone, it asks the same question.

**Email.** If Apple or Google stops sharing your email, we clear it. The account still works.

**Access and correction.** Email [privacy email] to ask for a copy of the account information we hold, or to correct it. We may ask you to sign in so we know the request is yours. The live conversation is not stored, so there is no transcript to send.

**California.** We do not sell personal information. We do not share it for cross-context behavioral advertising. You may use an authorized agent. We will not discriminate against you for asking.

**Europe, the UK, and Switzerland.** Where those laws apply, you can ask us to access, correct, delete, or restrict your information, and you can object to processing based on a legitimate interest. You can lodge a complaint with your local authority. [Counsel: add an Article 27 representative before the app is offered in the EEA.]

## 10. Security

We use access controls and encryption in transit. No method of sending data is perfectly secure. You are responsible for the Apple ID or Google account you use to sign in.

## 11. Changes

If we change this policy in a way that matters, we will show the new policy in the app before it applies, and we will change the effective date. Continued use after that notice is not how we collect consent for a new use of your speech. A new use of speech requires a new acceptance in the app.

## Draft notes (do not publish)

Read on 2026-10-28: [ELSA privacy notice](https://elsaspeak.com/en/privacy/) (March 2025), [Yoodli privacy policy](https://yoodli.ai/privacy-policy) (effective 2024-09-10), [Speechify privacy policy](https://speechify.com/privacy/) (updated July 22, 2026), [Speechify biometric policy](https://speechify.com/biometric-data-policy/), [xAI consumer privacy policy](https://x.ai/legal/privacy-policy) (effective August 24, 2026), and [xAI voice API docs](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/audio/voice).

Phrasing that was worth keeping, in our own words:

- Speechify’s opening promise is one sentence: they do not sell information, and ads are not the business. Section 1 of this draft does the same, because Orator’s business is the subscription.
- Yoodli splits collection into “you give us,” “we receive,” and “we generate.” Sections 2, 3, and 4 follow that split so a reader can see speech is not the same thing as an account row.
- ELSA names each recipient and the reason. Section 7 is a short table for the same reason. ELSA’s version is a paragraph that also covers ads, events, and research. Those recipients are not in this draft.

Phrasing that was not kept:

- ELSA says continued use of the service is consent, and that they may use voice recordings to improve the technology. Yoodli stores recordings and, on lower plans, may use session data to improve its models. Speechify’s consumer policy still has cookies, marketing email, and a broad “improve our algorithms” line. None of that matches the account design.
- ELSA’s age line is 16. xAI’s consumer line is 13. Orator’s line is 18, and the stored fact is a timestamp, not a birth date.
- Speechify’s biometric policy is about voice cloning. Orator does not clone a voice or make a voiceprint. The draft says that as a fact. It does not say “we comply with BIPA.” Counsel decides whether Illinois or Washington still needs a separate consent screen because a voice was sent to xAI at all.
- xAI’s consumer policy says user content may be used to train models, and it says that policy does not apply to the API. The voice API page says audio is processed in real time and is not stored or used for training. Section 3 states both, and does not promise that we control xAI.

Counsel still has to fill:

- Legal name, address, privacy email, effective date.
- How long `financial_retention` is kept.
- SB 243 wording for the AI partner and the 988 screen. The draft only says the partner is software and that the screen points at 988.
- Whether a biometric consent is required in Illinois or Washington.
- An EU representative, if the app is offered in the EEA.
- The xAI API contract, if it disagrees with the public “not stored, not used for training” line.

This draft describes the launch design. The xAI organization key is a Worker secret. It is not in the app.

Added 2026-10-30: speech-to-text is copied through the Worker because xAI does not offer a short-lived token for that socket. Conversation audio is not copied through the Worker. The published table must not say Cloudflare never receives audio.
